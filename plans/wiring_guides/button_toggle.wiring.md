# Wiring: button_toggle

A bouncy active-low button on D2 toggles the LED on D13 within 20 ms and prints ON/OFF on Serial1 at 9600 baud; bounce must be ignored.

Plan: `button_toggle.json`  
Pin map: `config/pinmap.yaml` (hash `46b391a6`)

| # | Nano 33 BLE pin | Nucleo pin (header) | STM32 | Resistor | Direction | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND | GND | GND | direct wire | common | ground |
| 2 | D0 | D8 | PA9 | 1 kΩ in series | rig drives | Serial1 RX ← rig (not used by this plan; wired with the pair) |
| 3 | D1 | D2 | PA10 | direct wire | rig measures | Serial1 TX → rig |
| 4 | D2 | D7 | PA8 | 1 kΩ in series | rig drives | button input (driven by the rig); delay start (edge watched); digital level (traced) |
| 5 | D13 | D3 | PB3 | direct wire | rig measures | response output (delay measured); digital level (traced) |
| 6 | RST | A1 | PA1 | direct wire | rig drives | reset line (the rig resets the Nano) |

## Checklist

1. Unplug both USB cables before wiring.
2. Connect GND first.
3. Both boards use 3.3 V logic. Never connect a Nucleo 5V or VIN pin to the Nano.
4. Put each listed resistor in series, in the wire itself (not to ground). It limits the current if both boards drive the line at once.
5. Leave every other Nano pin unconnected. Don't also connect a real button, LED or sensor to a listed pin.
6. The rig releases the pin when the button is not pressed; the student's `INPUT_PULLUP` holds it high. Don't add a pull-up resistor.
7. TX goes to RX: Nano D1 (Serial1 TX) → the rig's receive pin (Nucleo D2, PA10), and the rig's transmit pin (Nucleo D8, PA9) → Nano D0 (Serial1 RX).
8. Plug both boards into the laptop: the Nano by its own USB, the Nucleo by its ST-LINK USB.
9. Run `python scripts/rig_check.py`. It should report a matching pin map (`pinmap=46b391a6`). This checks the firmware, **not** the wires.
